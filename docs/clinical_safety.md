## 1. Toxicity Risk Index & Emergency Protocols

Herbalist AI enforces strict visual gating for species classified under high or extreme toxicity levels:

```
[Level 0 - None]    -> Completely edible / non-toxic culinary herbs (e.g. Ocimum basilicum).
[Level 1 - Low]     -> Generally recognized as safe in moderate culinary or topical doses (e.g. Aloe vera).
[Level 2 - Moderate]-> Therapeutic at micro-doses; toxic in excessive quantities or without processing (e.g. Rheum emodi).
[Level 3 - High]    -> Contains potent bio-alkaloids capable of inducing cardiac arrest or neurotoxicity (e.g. Aconitum, Datura, Hyoscyamus).
[Level 4 - Extreme] -> Lethal in sub-milligram quantities (e.g. Abrus precatorius seeds [abrin]).
```

| Species | Compound | Clinical Pathology | UI Handling |

| **Abrus precatorius** | Abrin | Ribosome-inactivating protein; fatal gastrointestinal & hepatic failure | Full-screen red alert; poison center banner |

| **Colchicum luteum** | Colchicine | Mitotic poison; multi-organ failure in overdose | Clinical prescription only flag |

## 2. Himalayan Conservation & Legal Regulations

Certain medicinal plants indigenous to the Kashmir Valley are subject to international and Indian national conservation laws:

- **CITES Appendix I**: *Saussurea costus* (Kuth). International commercial trade of wild specimens is strictly prohibited.
- **Wildlife Protection Act (India), Schedule VI**: Forbids extraction of specified endangered taxa without official forestry permits.
- **Good Agricultural and Collection Practices (GACP)**: Farmers are directed to cultivated ex-situ nursery stocks rather than wild habitat despoliation.
